# Critique: coverage, dataset pass

Tests review/critique-coverage-theory.md §5 (H1–H12) and §3 (G1–G12) against decisions/corpus-v0-tagged.jsonl (82 records). Script: review/analyse_corpus.py (`python3 -I`). Every number below is in review/corpus-metrics.json. Brackets are Wilson 95% intervals. **Persona** = `personaRelevance` high + medium (n = 45); **Low** = low (n = 37); **Git-P** = git-file records within Persona (n = 23).

## Executive summary

- **The corpus is committee-heavy, and "persona-relevant" doesn't mean "small group".** Where participant counts are known (55/82), the median is 11. Only 3 records have ≤3 participants, and 52% come from formal-process collectors (TC39, Rust, Go/Python). The persona stratum has a median of 10 participants. It is persona-relevant by _content_, not by group size, and it is mostly product-UI threads (all webfetch) plus ADRs. Read every rate as "teams debating in public", which is an upper bound for the coordination gaps and only a hint for a solo builder.
- **Verdicts.** SUPPORTED: H2, H5, H6, H7, H9, H10. REFUTED: H3 (on the options half), H11. INCONCLUSIVE: H1, H4, H8, H12.
- **The main coverage gap is the end state, not the shapes.** Real decisions rarely end as one clean answer. In the Persona stratum:
  - 69% [54–80] leave follow-up structure behind: sub-questions, "accepted in principle, details later", or stated consequences and obligations.
  - 42% [29–57] end in a composite answer: two or more options chosen, or one adopted only in part.
  - 29% [18–43] are parked or closed without a verdict.

  The design has one answer and an Open/Settled binary. All three findings hold on git-file records: 69%, 50% and 29%.
- **Shapes: keep four, and add no fifth.**
  - Choice is 67% of records and tune is 5% (24% in UI and product).
  - All 9 "mixed" records are bundles of coupled sub-choices. That's a structure problem (a parent question with children), not a missing shape.
  - Check is the primary shape in only 6% of records, but appears as primary or secondary in 32%. It behaves like evidence attached to other questions.
  - Shape κ is 0.618, with chance agreement of 0.60. Both recoding disagreements moved _to_ choice. Choice's dominance is partly an effect of how the corpus was selected and of the schema.
- **(a) Make now:**
  1. Follow-ups on Settle.
  2. A fate for each option in choice.
  3. A Parked state with a revisit trigger.
  4. Research and Spike on every shape.
  5. Reopen keeps the old answer as superseded.
  6. A "Driven by" field for outside authorities.
  7. Recording a decision after the fact, with a source link.
- **(b) Defer, but design for:** handing a question to another owner, team settles with an open-objection warning, coupled and blocking links, user-signal evidence, reframe history, convention scope, and image options.
- **(c) Out of scope:** votes and tallies, unanimity and blocking mechanics, stage gates, FCP and review boards, escalation, moderation, and release-train deadlines.
- **Over-built:**
  - Tune's bespoke preview: no git-file record is tune-shaped.
  - Tallies: votes or polls appear in 8.5% of records.
  - Escalation: present in 7% of records.
  - Takes are _not_ over-built: dissent survives in 76% of decided records with ≥3 participants.
- **What the corpus can't test:**
  - G1 (ratify Chopin's proposal) and G3 (calls agents make in code), because undebated decisions are excluded by construction.
  - H4, because option order isn't chronological.
  - Confidence, because it was coded inconsistently: 0% for the ADR collector against 75% for TC39.

## 4. What to do

### (a) Make now: relevant to the persona, and the evidence is strong

1. **Follow-ups on Settle** (#1): a consequences or obligations field, plus child questions linked to the parent, and a "settled in principle" parent whose children are still open. This is the single biggest gap, it holds on git-file, and it resolves "mixed".
2. **A fate for each option in choice** (#2): chosen / partly / later / not this, each with an optional reason. This replaces single-select and absorbs rank, partition and the reasons for rejected options.
3. **A Parked state with a revisit trigger** (#3, #7): it doesn't block Build plan. "Close without deciding", with a reason, replaces deleting a moot question. One "Revisit if…" field serves both Parked and provisional Settle. Split reversibility out of confidence.
4. **Research and Spike on every shape** (#4): per option on choice, and claims attachable to cells. This revisits hard call G for evidence actions.
5. **Reopen keeps the old answer as superseded** (#8): a forward link from the old answer, and revert PRs in Built into. This is nearly free, since the timeline is already kept.
6. **A "Driven by" field** (#6), for authorities outside Chopin.
7. **Record a decision after the fact** (#5): from a Chat message or PR, with a "Decided in" source. This is also the cheapest way into G3, agent-made calls, once build logs can be tested.

Keep G1 (Accept Chopin's proposal) from the theory pass. The corpus neither supports nor refutes it.

### (b) Design for, but defer: these matter for teams

- **Owner and decider roles**, with hand-over (#11). An approver comes later.
- **Team settle** and an **unanswered-objection warning** on Settle (#12).
- **Depends on / affects links** beyond parent and child (#9).
- **A user-signal evidence type** (#10). Confirm it on full-fidelity data first.
- **Reframe history** and Chopin-proposed reshape (#13).
- **Scope: repo**, exported to agent instructions (#14). Promote it to (a) if the local dataset shows ≥15% of decisions are rules in small repos.
- **Image options** (#15).
- **An optional "decide by" date** (#18).

### (c) Out of scope: committee and standards-body mechanics

## 5. Shapes

| Measure                                 | All                                                                | Persona             | Git-file              |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| choice / explore / mixed / check / tune | 67 / 11 / 11 / 6 / 5%                                              | 71 / 9 / 4 / 7 / 9% | 75 / 10 / 10 / 4 / 0% |
| Has a secondary shape                   | 67%                                                                | 58%                 | 60%                   |
| Check as primary or secondary           | 32%                                                                | 27%                 | 35%                   |
| Commonest pairs                         | choice alone 26; choice+check 19; explore↔choice 15; choice↔tune 8 |                     |                       |

- **Keep four shapes** (moderate confidence).
  - The corpus offers no candidate fifth shape: rank and partition are 4%.
  - Go/no-go decisions (existence and ban, 40% of records) were all coded as choice. Only 17% of those have ≤3 options, so a compact Yes / No / Not now control isn't evidenced as necessary. It's still plausible for the persona.
- **"Mixed" needs structure, not a shape.** Each of the 9 mixed records is a headline decision with attached sub-choices: [slog](https://github.com/golang/go/discussions/54763), [RFC 3392](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/pull/3392), [RFC 2394](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/pull/2394). Follow-ups (a1) make the parent one shape and each child its own shape.
- **Choice is probably over-dominant because of how it was coded** (moderate confidence). Four things point that way:
  - The admission rule requires ≥2 options weighed.
  - The schema forces an `options` array.
  - Both recoding disagreements went to choice (tune→choice, explore→choice).
  - Chance agreement is 0.60, so κ = 0.618 rests on 11/13 raw agreement.

  The correct reading is that "a decision with named alternatives is the commonest _public_ decision". For a solo builder with an agent, explore and accept-a-proposal may well be commoner, and this corpus can't say.
- **Check is better seen as evidence.** Choice+check is the second-commonest pair. This backs a4: claims attach to choice rows, and the standalone check card stays for real verify-a-belief questions (contentKind `verify-belief`: 1 record).
- **The κ caveat:** shape-level percentages carry an uncertainty of roughly ±10 points beyond sampling error. Don't make design decisions on gaps of less than 15 points between shapes.
